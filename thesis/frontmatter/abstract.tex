\begin{ttuabstract}
Quantum processors with more than a hundred qubits are now accessible through commercial clouds, yet none can complete a computation of practical value without a large amount of classical computation wrapped around it. This dissertation argues that the classical high-performance computing (HPC) surrounding a quantum processing unit (QPU) is a permanent, load-bearing part of the architecture, and it advances the state of the art at five layers of that co-designed stack. At the simulation layer, the Q-GEAR framework transforms Qiskit circuits gate-by-gate into CUDA-Q kernels through a compact three-tensor encoding and deploys them as containers on Slurm-managed GPU systems, simulating circuits of up to 42 qubits on 1,024 NVIDIA A100 GPUs of the Perlmutter supercomputer with roughly two orders of magnitude speed-up over a 128-core CPU node and about an order of magnitude over existing GPU simulators. At the algorithm layer, direct entanglement ansatz learning (DEAL) replaces black-box QAOA optimization with a direct mapping from QUBO coefficients to circuit angles, a calibration-driven dynamic physical mapping and adaptive digital zero-noise extrapolation, raising ground-state success on IBM Heron processors by up to 14\%, while the quantum approximate walk algorithm (QAWA) introduces a classical-data-traceable oracle of linear depth whose outputs are verifiable in polynomial time.

At the data layer, four learning primitives built on vectorized address--data encodings are developed and validated on superconducting and trapped-ion hardware: QPIE, a non-sequential hybrid network with parallel classical--quantum information exchange; VQT, a quantum transformer whose attention is computed by a vectorized quantum dot product with a learnable nonlinear encoder; ShardQ, a hardware-aware circuit cutting-and-knitting compiler that reduces encoding error by about 15\%; and NNQA, which compiles trained polynomial networks into exact quantum arithmetic reaching over 99.5\% accuracy for polynomials up to degree 35. At the synthesis layer, RubriQ frames fault-tolerant circuit synthesis as rubric-guided reinforcement learning of a code-generating language model with GPU simulation inside the reward loop, achieving 3.31$\times$ mean T-gate compression with fewer than 1\% hardware-constraint violations, and a unified cost-and-error model for direct quantum simulation of nonlinear waves quantifies where measurement and reload costs overtake the classical alternative.

At the trust layer, a bounded cloud fault model and the notion of accepted logical disturbance make it possible to audit the exposure of error-correcting encoders to a co-located fault-injection adversary; a polynomial-cost seeded Clifford ensemble reduces mean accepted disturbance on the five-qubit code by 86.7\%, and a post-quantum cryptographic module designed to the FIPS 140-3 requirements supplies the validated randomness and key-establishment services that the audit and the rest of the stack consume. Together these contributions show that gains at each classical layer compound, and that the path to useful quantum computation runs through the whole stack.
\end{ttuabstract}
