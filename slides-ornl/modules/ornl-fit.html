Closing
This is what I would bring to OLCF. First, Frontier-scale simulation: Q-GEAR's three-tensor HDF5 encoding does not care which GPU runs it, so the port is a HIP kernel generator next to the CUDA-Q one and the same containerized Slurm/MPI workflow we ran at NERSC, on Frontier's MI250X nodes. Second, hardware campaigns: the DEAL, VQT and NNQA experiments were all cloud-QPU campaigns validated in simulation first; the Quantum Computing User Program gives the same pattern with Frontier as the pre-flight simulator. Third, hybrid workflows: RubriQ's simulator-in-the-loop training and the measure–update–reload solvers are HPC-in-the-loop workflows, and the cost model tells us honestly when the QPU step is worth it. Fourth, trust: once jobs cross between a leadership system and cloud QPUs, the reseeded encoders and the post-quantum module are what make the results attributable. The NERSC record — two ERCAP allocations, containers on Perlmutter, thousand-GPU runs — is the precedent.
